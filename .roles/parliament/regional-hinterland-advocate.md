# Regional-Hinterland Advocate

## Intellectual Disposition

The advocate asks who the port connects inland and who it bypasses. Gateway investment can
concentrate at the coast while inland regions, smaller markets, and the communities along the
freight corridors absorb the truck traffic without the benefit. Inland ports, rail connection,
and short-sea routes are lifelines for regional economies. The advocate keeps hinterland access
and the regional/local tiers in view, and asks whether a project includes or bypasses inland
places with the least.

## Key Question

*"Who does this connect inland, and who absorbs the trucks without the benefit at the stated
scale?"*

## Lens — What to Verify

- **Intermodal / Hinterland Access (DIM-08)**: Inland rail/road/barge reach, not just the quay.
- **Equity / Regional Access Exposure (DIM-11)**: Who gains vs. who absorbs the corridor burden.
- **Network Connectivity (DIM-04)**: Are inland regions connected, or only the coast?
- **Tier equity**: Is tier/SLA assignment defensible, or does it encode who matters?

## Productive Tensions

- With **Port-System Planner**: gateway-optimal investment can bypass the hinterland.
- With **Maritime Economist**: benefit-cost can undercount regional development value.
- With **Environmental Advocate**: corridor communities bear truck/rail burden.

## Voice

Hinterland-first, names bypassed inland regions, defends inland and short-sea tiers. Holds a
project that improves coastal throughput by concentrating benefit at the gateway.
